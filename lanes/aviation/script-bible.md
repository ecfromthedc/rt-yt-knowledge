# Aviation Lane — Script Bible

The reusable template for how the winning aviation-disaster channels actually build a video. Every rule below is grounded in the two teardowns in this lane: **@MentourPilot** and **@GreenDotAviation**. Both channels run a million-view baseline and a live power law — MentourPilot's outliers pull **1.7x** the channel average, GreenDotAviation's pull **1.9x**. This is a single-genre lane: *the air-crash investigation story, told as a mystery*. Build to that genre and nothing else.

## 0. The Core Genre Thesis

Every winning video in this lane is the same product: **a known catastrophe re-told as an unfolding mystery, where the audience already knows the plane is in trouble but does NOT know why, who's at fault, or whether anyone survives.** The viewer is not watching for the ending (the crash is in the title). They're watching to understand the *chain* — the sequence of small decisions and failures that made the outcome inevitable. The script's entire job is to ration out that chain so the curiosity gap never fully closes until the final third.

This is why the format supports 24–66 minute runtimes (GreenDotAviation Avianca 052 = 50m; MentourPilot Mount Erebus = 66m) and still holds APV. Length is not the enemy; an unresolved chain is the retention engine.

## 1. Title Formulas (the reusable money-makers)

Both channels converge on the same handful of structures. Use these as fill-in-the-blank templates. Every title pairs an **emotional/curiosity trigger** with the **flight identifier** (airline + flight number), usually split by a `|` or `..`.

### Formula A — The Question Hook (GreenDotAviation's signature)
`What HAPPENED [Airline/Flight]??` · `Why did BOTH engines fail immediately after takeoff?? | SAS 751` · `What were they THINKING?? | Gulf Air Flight 072`
- Open with an interrogative (What / Why / How), end on a **double question mark**. The `??` is doing real CTR work — it signals "even the experts are confused."
- Proven instances: *What HAPPENED Emirates 521??* (1.57M), *Why did BOTH engines fail immediately after takeoff?? | SAS 751* (937K).

### Formula B — The Impossible/Contradiction Hook
`This Should NOT Have Been POSSIBLE! | [Flight]` · `Crashing after 22 seconds?? Delta 1141` · `The Plane that Couldn't Land | Avianca 052`
- State an outcome that violates the viewer's mental model of how flying works. The gap between "planes don't do that" and "this one did" is the click.
- Proven: *This Should NOT Have Been POSSIBLE! | LATAM 8073* (1.51M), *The Plane that Couldn't Land* (1.28M).

### Formula C — The Buried-Significance Hook (MentourPilot's signature)
`The Most Important Crash You've Never Heard Of... | [Flight]` · `What REALLY Happened To [Subject]?!`
- Promise that this is a *hidden* or *misunderstood* story — the viewer's ignorance is the hook. "You've Never Heard Of" and "What REALLY Happened" both imply the official version is wrong or incomplete.
- Proven: *The Most Important Crash You've Never Heard Of... | TWA Flight 514* (1.61M), *What REALLY Happened To Kobe Bryant's Helicopter?!* (1.70M — the channel's #1).

### Formula D — The Emotional-Stakes / Moral Hook
`The WORST Story I've Ever Told.. | [Flight]` · `This Cost Them EVERYTHING | [Flight]` · `A Litany of LIES! | [Event]` · `An Ultimate Sacrifice.. | The Heartbreaking Story of [Flight]`
- Front-load a value judgment (worst, lies, sacrifice, everything) that frames the story as morally charged, not just technical. This is what lets MentourPilot run 44–66 minute videos — the promise is emotional, not procedural.
- Proven: *The WORST Story I've Ever Told.. | Germanwings 9525* (1.67M), *A Litany of LIES! | The Mount Erebus Disaster* (1.07M).

### Formula E — The Visceral Image Hook
`Melting From The Inside Out! | Swiss Air 111` · `LOST at sea?? | The INSANE story of Flight 782` · `The Flight that went DARK | British Airways 870` · `Trapped! The harrowing story of Virgin Flight 024`
- Lead with a concrete, physical, slightly grotesque image (melting, lost at sea, went dark, trapped). The thumbnail can render this literally.
- Proven: *Melting From The Inside Out! | Swiss Air 111* (1.31M), *LOST at sea?? | Flight 782* (1.56M).

### Title rules (non-negotiable)
1. **ALWAYS include the flight identifier** (airline + number, or named subject). It signals "real event, documented, investigable" — the credibility anchor that separates this from clickbait.
2. **ONE capitalized power word minimum**, screamed: HAPPENED, WORST, DARK, TRAPPED, LIES, EVERYTHING, POSSIBLE, REALLY, THINKING. The teardowns show the top-view titles use *more* and *stronger* caps-verbs than the lower-view ones.
3. **Curiosity gap must be unresolved by the title.** The title says *what* happened (crash) but never *why*. "Why did BOTH engines fail" tells you the symptom, withholds the cause.
4. **Use `??` or `..` or `!`** as the closer — these punctuation tics are channel-wide and read as authentic enthusiast voice, not corporate.

## 2. Thumbnail Patterns

Per the Intrigue Doctrine (one idea per thumbnail) and the teardown title imagery:
- **One literal image of the title's visceral noun.** "Went DARK" → blacked-out cockpit/cabin. "LOST at sea" → aircraft over open water. "Melting" → fire/heat damage. Don't compose two ideas.
- **The aircraft in jeopardy, mid-event** — banking hard, smoke, night, weather — not a clean stock shot of a parked plane.
- **High-contrast emotional face or hand** when the story is human (MentourPilot's emotional hooks). For technical-mystery videos (GreenDotAviation), the *aircraft state* is the subject.
- **A short text overlay echoing the power word**, not the full title. Reinforce, don't repeat.
- Thumbnail and title must contrast, not duplicate — title carries the question, thumbnail carries the image.

## 3. Cold-Open Structure (first 0:00–0:30)

Both teardowns describe an identical cold-open machine. Build it in this order:

1. **0:00–0:05 — Drop into the crisis, mid-action.** Do not introduce the channel, the host, or the date first. Open *inside* the tense moment: the aircraft is already in trouble, the scenario is already dramatic. GreenDotAviation: "they all start with a dramatic and attention-grabbing scenario." This is the <15s hook from the Field Manual.
2. **0:05–0:20 — Open the loop / pose the unanswerable.** State the mystery as a question the viewer cannot answer yet: *Why was this possible? How did trained pilots miss this? What were they thinking?* MentourPilot: "leave questions unanswered." Give *just enough* information to make the question land, and no more.
3. **0:20–0:30 — Plant the stakes + the promise.** Signal why this story matters beyond itself ("the most important crash," "this changed aviation forever," "this should never have happened"). MentourPilot explicitly "suggests the importance of the story for a broader audience." This is the stakes-by-0:30 rule.
4. **End the cold open on a cliffhanger**, then cut — to the title card / channel ident / the rewind-to-the-beginning transition. Never resolve anything in the cold open.

**Rule:** the cold open answers nothing. It only sharpens the question. If a viewer could click away satisfied after 60 seconds, the cold open failed.

## 4. Narrative Arc (the body)

After the cold open, the proven structure is a **chronological chain-of-failures rewind**:

1. **Reset to calm.** Rewind to before the flight — introduce the aircraft, the crew, the route, the conditions. Humanize lightly. This contrast (calm → catastrophe) is what makes the later beats hit.
2. **Lay the dominoes one at a time.** Walk the timeline forward. Each beat introduces ONE contributing factor (a maintenance shortcut, a weather call, a miscommunication, a system quirk). Crucially: **let the audience see each domino before the crew does.** Dramatic irony is the retention engine — the viewer is shouting at the screen.
3. **The point of no return.** Mark the specific moment the outcome became unavoidable. Name it explicitly ("from this second, the crash was inevitable").
4. **The event.** The crash/emergency itself, told beat-by-beat with the chain now fully visible.
5. **The investigation / the reveal.** Resolve the title's question. *This* is where the curiosity gap finally closes — never earlier. For MentourPilot's "LIES"/"REALLY Happened" framing, the reveal is often that the official story was wrong.
6. **The legacy beat.** What changed because of this — the regulation, the procedure, the lives later saved. This pays off the "most important crash" promise and gives the emotional landing.

**Pacing law:** one new fact per beat, each beat raising a new micro-question as it answers the last. Never dump the full timeline at once. The 24–66 min runtimes survive *only* because the chain is rationed.

## 5. Tonal Register

- **Authoritative but accessible.** These channels (esp. MentourPilot, a real airline pilot) win on credibility. Use correct terminology, then immediately translate it. Never condescend; never lose the layperson.
- **Sober and respectful**, not gory or exploitative. Real people died. The drama comes from the *chain of decisions*, not from dwelling on death. MentourPilot's "Heartbreaking," "Ultimate Sacrifice," "Litany of Lies" framing treats victims with gravity.
- **Empathetic to the crew.** The recurring move is "here's why a competent, trained pilot made this fatal choice" — not "the pilots were idiots." This is what makes the dramatic irony land as tragedy, not mockery.
- **Quietly urgent.** Steady narration, but the present-tense, "you are there" framing keeps tension high.

## 6. Recurring Phrases / Verbal Tics (steal these)

- Framing the mystery: *"What REALLY happened…"*, *"What were they thinking?"*, *"This should never have been possible."*, *"The most important crash you've never heard of."*
- Marking stakes: *"This changed aviation forever."*, *"This cost them everything."*, *"From this moment, there was no going back."*
- Dramatic-irony setups: *"What the crew didn't know was…"*, *"At this point, everything still looked normal."*, *"But there was a problem no one had noticed."*
- Investigation reveals: *"When investigators finally examined the wreckage…"*, *"The truth was far worse than anyone expected."*, *"It was a litany of lies."*

## 7. Transition Tactics

- **Question-bridge transitions.** End each section on the micro-question the next section answers: *"But how could a modern airliner simply vanish? To understand that, we have to go back."*
- **The rewind cut.** Cold open → "to understand how we got here, we need to start at the beginning" → reset to calm. This is the single most reliable structural transition in the lane.
- **Time-stamp markers.** "14 minutes before impact," "T-minus 22 seconds" (cf. *Crashing after 22 seconds?? Delta 1141*) — countdown framing accelerates the back half and reminds the viewer the clock is running.
- **"And then…" escalation.** Chain dominoes with simple temporal connectors that imply inevitability building.

## 8. CTA Patterns

- **No hard CTA in the first 60 seconds** — protect the hook and early retention. Nothing competes with the open loop.
- **Mid-roll soft CTA** placed at a natural breath (after the "point of no return," before the event) — a single line: like/subscribe framed as "so you don't miss the next investigation." Keep it under 8 seconds.
- **End-card CTA tied to the next mystery.** Close on the legacy beat, then point to a *related* incident video ("if you found this disturbing, the story of [Flight X] is even worse"). The CTA is itself a curiosity gap into the next watch — this is what compounds session time and feeds the power law.
- **Never break tone for the CTA.** It stays sober and in-voice; no jarring "hey guys smash that like button."

## 9. Operator Quick-Build Checklist

1. Pick a documented incident with an unresolved-feeling *why* and a clear legacy/change.
2. Write the title from Formula A–E: trigger word(s) + `??`/`..`/`!` + flight identifier. Confirm the *why* is withheld.
3. Build one literal thumbnail of the title's visceral noun. One idea only.
4. Cold open: crisis mid-action (0:05) → unanswerable question (0:20) → stakes/promise (0:30) → cliffhanger cut. Resolve nothing.
5. Rewind to calm, then lay dominoes one per beat with dramatic irony (viewer sees each before the crew).
6. Mark the point of no return → the event → the investigation reveal (gap closes here) → the legacy beat.
7. One new fact per beat; each beat opens a fresh micro-question.
8. Soft mid-roll CTA at a breath; end-card CTA into the next mystery.
9. Tone check: authoritative, accessible, sober, empathetic to crew. No mockery, no gore.
10. QC against Field Manual gates: CTR ≥10% (title/thumb), APV ≥30% (chain rationing), retention flat-to-rising through 0:30 (cold-open machine).
