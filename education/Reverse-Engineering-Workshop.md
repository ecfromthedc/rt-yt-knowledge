# The Reverse-Engineering & Intrigue Workshop

> You already learned the *what* in the Field Manual: CTR ≥10%, APV ≥30%, hook under 15s, stakes by 0:30, titles = a curiosity gap, thumbnails = one idea. This workshop teaches the *how* — how to take a teardown of a proven winner and convert their patterns into **your own** titles, thumbnails, and hooks without copying.

The entire RT YouTube method is one sentence: **we don't guess, we reverse-engineer.** Somebody already paid the tuition — they shipped 200 videos, the algorithm sorted the winners from the losers, and the teardown hands you the answer key. Your job is not to invent intrigue from scratch. Your job is to read the answer key and re-skin the winning move into your own niche.

This is the most important skill on the whole team, because it's the one that compounds. A good script is one good video. A good reverse-engineering instinct is every video forever.

---

## Part 1 — How to read a teardown

Every teardown file in `$SEED/lanes/<lane>/teardowns/` has the same five sections. Read them in this order and you'll extract the signal fast.

**Section 1 — The numbers.** Skip past channel average. Go straight to the *outlier multiple*. On `@FallofCivilizations` the top videos pull **6.2x** the channel average. On `@MentourPilot` it's **1.7x**. That multiple tells you how violent the power law is in your lane. A 6.2x channel means a handful of titles carry the whole channel — so the title is *everything*. A 1.7x channel means the floor is high and consistency matters more. History operators live and die by the breakout title; aviation operators win by being reliably good across many.

**Section 2 — Top videos.** This is the answer key. Don't read it as a list of videos — read it as a list of **moves**. The Sumerians video didn't win because Sumerians are interesting. It won because of the *packaging move*: a dead, forgotten civilization framed as "the Fall of the First Cities." Same move, different costume, every time.

**Section 3 — Title patterns.** The teardown has already done the extraction for you: recurring structures, power words, and crucially, *what the winners do that the losers don't*. That last line is gold. On Kings & Generals, the winners "provide a clear, specific outcome (Saved Australia, Failing)" while the losers stay vague. That's a rule you can apply tonight.

**Section 4 — Hook patterns.** The opening 60 seconds, decoded. Notice these are nearly identical across lanes: introduce the situation, open a loop, withhold the payoff, end on a cliffhanger. The Intrigue Doctrine in the wild.

**Section 5 — The steal list.** "Copy: the title formula + cold-open structure. Beat them on: packaging contrast + upload cadence." This is the operating instruction. You copy the *mechanism*. You compete on *execution*.

> **The one rule that keeps us legal and original:** copy the **mechanism**, never the **content**. The mechanism is "superlative + specific event + withheld outcome." The content is "Kobe Bryant's helicopter." You take the mechanism into your own niche. That's reverse-engineering. Lifting the content is just theft, and the algorithm punishes the second guy anyway.

## Part 2 — The four intrigue mechanisms (extracted from the real teardowns)

Across all four lanes, every winning title runs one of four mechanisms. Here they are with the actual winning titles that prove them.

### Mechanism A — The Withheld Outcome
*Name the event, hide the result. The brain cannot tolerate an open loop.*

Real winners:
- `@GreenDotAviation` — **"What HAPPENED Emirates 521??"** (1.57M views)
- `@MentourPilot` — **"What REALLY Happened To Kobe Bryant's Helicopter?!"** (1.7M views)
- `@Fireship` — **"Claude just got another superpower..."** (the "..." *is* the withheld outcome)

The trailing `??` and `...` are not lazy punctuation — they are the visual signature of an unclosed loop. The viewer clicks to close it.

### Mechanism B — The Superlative + Specific
*Make an extreme claim, then anchor it to one concrete subject so it reads as true, not clickbait.*

Real winners:
- `@MentourPilot` — **"The Most Important Crash You've Never Heard Of... | TWA Flight 514"**
- `@MentourPilot` — **"This Should NOT Have Been POSSIBLE! | LATAM 8073"**
- `@FallofCivilizations` — **"The Assyrians - Empire of Iron"** ("Empire of Iron" is the superlative; "The Assyrians" is the anchor)

Notice the structure: **[extreme emotional claim] | [specific named subject]**. The pipe character does real work — it lets you make a wild promise on the left and prove you're serious on the right.

### Mechanism C — The Pattern Interrupt / Contradiction
*State something that shouldn't be possible. The contradiction creates the gap.*

Real winners:
- `@Fireship` — **"This new Linux distro is breaking the law, by design…"**
- `@Fireship` — **"Google just casually disrupted the open-source AI narrative…"** ("casually" + "disrupted" is the interrupt)
- `@GreenDotAviation` — **"Crashing after 22 seconds?? Delta 1141"** (planes don't crash in 22 seconds — that's the contradiction)
- `@KingsAndGenerals` — **"Japan's First Major Land Defeat — The Battle That Saved Australia"** (Japan losing on land *and* it saving Australia — two surprises)

### Mechanism D — The Promised Transformation
*Don't tease a story — promise the viewer a state change. Used heavily in meditation, but works anywhere outcome-driven.*

Real winners:
- `@jasonstephensonmeditation` — **"Fall Asleep in MINUTES! Guided Meditation for Instant Sleep"**
- `@jasonstephensonmeditation` — **"You Are Safe, Supported and Cared For"** (the channel's #1 video, 403K — it promises a *feeling*, not a topic)
- `@jasonstephensonmeditation` — **"Feel Joy Again, Self-Care Sleep Meditation for Happiness and Positivity"**

The teardown's own note: the winners "include 'Safe, Supported and Cared For'" and "use more specific emotional triggers like 'Feel Joy Again.'" In meditation, the curiosity gap is a *promised relief* from a named pain.

## Part 3 — The conversion workflow (teardown → your title)

Five steps. Do them in order. This is the core craft.

1. **Pick the outlier, not the average.** Open Section 2. Take the top 1–2 videos by `vs avg` multiple. Ignore everything at 1.0x or below — those are baseline, not signal.
2. **Name the mechanism.** Which of the four (A/B/C/D) is it running? Usually it's obvious. Sometimes it stacks two (Kings & Generals stacks Superlative + Contradiction).
3. **Strip the content, keep the skeleton.** Rewrite the winning title as a fill-in-the-blank template. "What REALLY Happened To Kobe Bryant's Helicopter?!" becomes **"What REALLY Happened To [your specific subject]?!"**
4. **Pour in your own topic.** Drop your niche's subject into the skeleton. Make the specific subject *genuinely* specific — a named flight, a named civilization, a named model, a named feeling.
5. **Run the Field Manual checks.** Does it open a curiosity gap? Is it ONE idea (not three)? Could the thumbnail show a single image that pairs with it? If yes, ship it to the title pile. Write 5–10, never 1.

## Part 5 — Hands-on exercises

Do these with a real teardown open. Don't theorize — write actual titles.

**Exercise 1 — Mechanism spotting (10 min).**
Open `@MentourPilot`'s teardown, Section 2. For each of the top 5 titles, write A / B / C / D next to it. Then answer: which mechanism shows up most in this lane, and why does it fit aviation? (Hint: aviation is outcome-suspense — people want to know who lived.)

**Exercise 2 — Skeleton extraction (15 min).**
Take these three real winners and rewrite each as a fill-in-the-blank skeleton with the content stripped out:
- *"The WORST Story I've Ever Told.. | Germanwings 9525"*
- *"Why Greece Fell to Rome - Legion vs Phalanx: Why Rome Won"*
- *"You Are Safe, Supported and Cared For"*

**Exercise 3 — The 3-title drill (20 min) — the core rep.**
Pick the single highest-multiple title in YOUR lane's teardown. Name its mechanism. Then write **3 titles for your own channel** using its skeleton. Rules: each must be ONE idea, each must open a curiosity gap, each must name a specific subject. Bring all three to the next packaging review.

**Exercise 4 — Cross-pollinate (15 min).**
Steal a mechanism from a *different* lane than yours. Example: take Fireship's *"[emotional verdict]... [company] just [action]"* structure and write a **history** title with it. (e.g., *"Catastrophic. Rome just handed Carthage the entire Mediterranean."*) This is how you find packaging angles your competitors haven't tried — the teardown's "beat them on: packaging contrast" instruction in action.

**Exercise 5 — Hook conversion (20 min).**
Read Section 4 (hook patterns) of any teardown. Notice the universal cold-open shape: *situation → open loop → withhold → cliffhanger*. Now write the **first 15 seconds** of a script for one of your Exercise 3 titles. Stakes must land by 0:30. Read it aloud and time it. If you're still setting up context at 0:20, cut.

## Part 6 — The intrigue checklist (pin this to your wall)

Before any title leaves your hands, it must pass all six:

- [ ] **Outlier-sourced** — the skeleton came from a top-multiple video, not a guess.
- [ ] **Mechanism named** — you can say "this is A/B/C/D" out loud.
- [ ] **Curiosity gap open** — the viewer cannot predict the payoff from the title.
- [ ] **One idea only** — not two, not three. One.
- [ ] **Specific anchor** — a named subject (flight number, civilization, model, feeling), never generic.
- [ ] **Thumbnail-able** — one image can carry it; title and thumbnail say *different* things that combine.

And the two unbreakable laws underneath everything:

1. **Copy the mechanism, never the content.** The patterns are universal; the subjects are yours.
2. **Write 5–10, ship 1.** Volume at the title stage is free. Pick the best, kill the rest. Cheap-by-default — invest production only behind a title that already proved it can open a loop.

> You now have the answer key *and* the conversion engine. Every teardown in `$SEED/lanes/` is another stack of proven moves waiting to be re-skinned into your niche. Read one before every video. The craft of intrigue is not talent — it's reps against the answer key.
