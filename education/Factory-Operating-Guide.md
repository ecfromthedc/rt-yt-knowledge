# How the Autonomous Content Factory Works

> You're not here to make videos. You're here to **find what the algorithm already wants to reward and feed it faster than anyone else.** The factory exists so that the moment you spot a winner, copying it costs almost nothing. Read this once cover-to-cover. Then keep it open next to the Field Manual — that one teaches the craft (CTR, retention, intrigue), this one teaches the *machine* you operate.

The whole bet in one sentence: **launch ~18–20 cheap shots, read the signal, pour concrete on the 1–2 that pop.** The math says ~65% of our $83K/mo target comes from a small number of home-runs and strong winners — not from grinding out volume. So everything we've built serves one job: make shots cheap enough to take a lot of them, and catch the pop fast enough to capitalize before the window closes.

That splits the work into **two engines.**

---

## PART 1 — THE TWO ENGINES

Everything in the factory is one of these two things. Learn to tell them apart, because they ask different things of you.

### Engine 1 — THE GROWTH ENGINE (the chess)

This is the **only** place real human judgment lives in the system. It's the chess game: read the board, see which piece is over-indexing, and decide what to make next. You earn your 20% here. Nowhere else.

The Growth Engine is three moves on repeat:
1. **Read the over-index signal.** A video is *over-indexing* when it beats its own channel's recent baseline — on velocity (views vs the channel's usual), on CTR (packaging landing), or on retention (the content delivering). You don't hunt for "good numbers" in a vacuum; you hunt for a video out-running *its own channel*. A 6× spike on a 3-week-old channel is the single most valuable event in the whole portfolio — that's the algorithm telling you, out loud, what it wants to feed.
2. **Diagnose, don't guess.** Every pop lands on the CTR×APV grid (you know this from the Field Manual): high-CTR + high-APV = **make more, now**; high-CTR + low-APV = packaging worked, fix the hook; low-CTR + high-APV = great video nobody saw, re-package; low + low = wrong topic, kill it. The grid tells you the move. You don't improvise.
3. **Double down.** When something pops in the winning quadrant, you drop everything and make 2–3 more in that exact cluster *that week*. Early winners are the algorithm handing you the map. Follow the scent.

That's the chess. It's not about working harder — it's about reading the board correctly and putting your next shots where the signal points. The factory's job is to make that next shot cheap enough that following the scent is always affordable.

### Engine 2 — THE PRODUCTION ENGINE (the factory)

This is the assembly line, and it has exactly one design rule: **cheap and frictionless by default.** Not cheap as in "worse" — cheap as in *a v1 video is a complete, shippable, monetizable artifact.* We escalate production *up* from cheap only after a format proves itself. We never patch *down* from premium.

Why cheap-by-default matters: if a video costs $40 and 8 hours, you can only afford a handful of shots, and the power-law bet falls apart. If a video costs $1–12 and a couple hours, you can take 18–20 shots without flinching — and when one pops, cloning it costs the same cheap floor but with a proven blueprint. **The marginal cost of cloning a $15K/mo format approaches the marginal cost of a cheap shot.** That's the whole game.

Three things make the factory cheap:
- **Human judgment is spent only where it moves the needle** — topic pick, hook/title/thumbnail (the craft of intrigue), and the promote-to-invest call. Everything else trends toward full-auto.
- **A proven format is a *template*, not a memory.** When something wins, we freeze it into a folder a new channel can fork in under a day. The factory's value compounds in the template library, not in any one video.
- **Recurring cost is the enemy of volume.** Free archival footage by default. Gen-AI b-roll (Veo/Kling) is unlocked *only* on proven formats. TTS is one click. Every dollar of per-video spend has to be earned by a signal.

**The mental shortcut:** Growth = *what do we make next?* (judgment). Production = *make it cheap and fast* (plumbing). When you're staring at a dashboard deciding where to point your shots, you're playing chess. When you're opening a 90%-built timeline and trimming, you're working the factory.

## PART 2 — THE END-TO-END PIPELINE (one loop)

Here's the whole thing as one circle. Follow it once and the rest of this guide clicks into place.

```
  ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │                                                                  │
  │   SCENT          DRAFT         ASSEMBLE        SHIP              │
  │   Research &  →  Closed-Loop →  Production  →  Upload            │
  │   Teardown      Scripting      Engine          (Stage 09)        │
  │   Swarm (03)    (04)           (01)                              │
  │      ▲                                            │              │
  │      │                                            ▼              │
  │   Corpus  ←──  WINNER-LOG  ←──  Chess Board  ←──  Analytics      │
  │   (06)         the lesson       Dashboard (02)    (Stage 10)     │
  │   refills      logged           reads the signal                 │
  │   the cluster                                                    │
  │                                                                  │
  └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Stage by stage, in plain language:

1. **Scent — find a validated topic.** The Research & Teardown Swarm validates the niche (is it evergreen *and* rising?), tears down the competitors who already won, and keeps a standing queue of ranked topics with hook angles attached. **You never stare at a blank page.** You open a pre-ranked queue and approve one.
2. **Draft — write against the Bible, not from scratch.** Each channel has a *Script Bible* (its voice, structure, pacing, hook doctrine) generated once at launch. The scripting chain drafts a script *against* that Bible and the hook you picked. You skim-edit; you don't write from zero.
3. **Assemble — open a 90%-done timeline.** Voiceover renders in one click (ElevenLabs). Beat-tagged b-roll gets pulled from the script automatically (free archival by default). The Premiere + Claude integration lays VO + music + footage onto a timeline from a manifest. **You open a finished-looking timeline and trim — never an empty one.**
4. **Package — the craft-of-intrigue gate.** This is where your human touch matters most. Pick 1 of 3 AI-generated thumbnail concepts. Pick 1 of 5 curiosity-gap titles, scored on doctrine. This is the highest-leverage 15 minutes of your week.
5. **Ship — upload.** *(Stage 09 — currently the one manual step; auto-publish is the top build priority.)* Today you upload + paste metadata by hand. Soon: the substrate publishes for you.
6. **Read the signal.** *(Stage 10 — feeds the dashboard.)* CTR/APV/retention/velocity flow back. The Chess Board watches for over-indexing and fires a **Double-Down Card** when a video pops.
7. **Log the winner.** When something over-indexes, it goes into the **winner-log** — so the lesson belongs to the *whole portfolio*, not one operator's head.
8. **Refill the cluster.** The Swarm re-points at the winning angle and queues 2–3 clone-ready topics. The Production Engine clones the format cheaply. And you're back at step 1 — but now pointed where the signal said to go.

That loop is the company. Everything else is in service of running it faster and cheaper.

**The "cheap shot → proof → invest" ladder** governs how much you spend at each turn:
- **Rung 1 — Cheap shot.** Default for all ~18–20 channels. AI-assisted script, one-click VO, free archival b-roll, auto-assembly, 3 thumbnail concepts. **$1–12, 1–6 hours.** Ship 2/week/channel and read signal.
- **Rung 2 — Signal.** A format earns promotion only when the dashboard shows it over-indexing. We do **not** invest on faith. A format must throw a signal first — that's what makes 18–20 cheap shots affordable.
- **Rung 3 — Invest.** Now it pays for itself: human writer pass, Veo/Kling gen-AI b-roll, paid editor polish, designer thumbnails, clone to sibling channels. The format's RPM × volume funds the spend.

(Aviation is the one exception — mystery-doc is inherently home-run-shaped, so it starts near invest-tier. Every *other* lane earns its way up the ladder.)

## PART 3 — THE SUBSTRATE + THE SKILLS (the machine under the hood)

You don't have to build any of this. But you should understand the two pieces of machinery you'll touch every week.

### The substrate — *a video is a state machine, not a file*

The source of truth is **Postgres (`rt-yt-substrate`)**. Every video is a *row* that moves through states — research → script → VO → assembly → packaged → published → measured. No state lives in someone's head, a Drive folder, or a DM thread. This matters because it's *why* the factory can be automated: every stage reads and writes the same row, so a machine (or the next operator) always knows exactly where a video is.

Alongside it sits the **corpus (`rt-yt-knowledge`)** — the shared brain. If the substrate is the *machine* and the playbook is the *method*, the corpus is the *memory*. It holds, per lane:
- **teardowns/** — competitor channel breakdowns (the steal list)
- **script-bible.md** — each channel's voice and structure
- **winner-log/** — the cross-lane log of what over-indexed and *why*
- **format-library/** — reusable, lane-agnostic format patterns

**Empty memory = every operator relearns the same lesson.** The two iron rules: every teardown uses the template, and every over-indexer gets a winner-log entry. That consistency is what lets the agent swarm query the corpus and lets one operator's discovery become every operator's edge.

### The skills — your power tools

Skills are one-command tools that do the grunt work and hand you back something to judge. The headline one is already built and working:

**`/teardown` — competitor reverse-engineering, already live.** Point it at a competitor channel and a lane, and it: pulls the channel's outlier videos (where the money is), grabs **captions** for the top performers (instant — no slow transcription), and runs **local Ollama** to extract their title formulas, power words, hook structures, and the exact curiosity-gap mechanism they use. It writes a finished teardown doc straight into the corpus format. You review it and fill the "steal list" — what to copy, what to beat them on.

> *It really works.* Run today against @KingsandGenerals, it pulled 13 videos, found the outlier doing **2.8×** the channel average (*"Japan's First Major Land Defeat — The Battle That Saved Australia,"* 387K views), and extracted their title formula (`<Country>'s First Major [Event] - [Outcome]`), their power words (*First, Last stand, Failed, Impossible*), and their cold-open structure (broad context → narrow to a key figure → cliffhanger). That's a competitor's playbook, reverse-engineered in minutes, ready for you to clone.

Try it:
```
python3 teardown.py --channel @VoicesofthePast --lane history
python3 teardown.py --channel https://www.youtube.com/@Fireship --lane ai-dev --top 12
```

The sibling skills follow the same pattern — built or building under `rt-yt-substrate`: **`/niche-validate`** (is this niche evergreen *and* rising?), **`/topic-loop`** (ranked topic + title queues), **`/script-bible`** (your channel's voice, generated once), **`/assemble`** + **`/vo-render`** + **`/broll-fetch`** (the 90%-done-timeline machine), **`/thumb`** (3 concepts against intrigue doctrine), **`/winner-log`** (log the pop), and **`/format-extract`** (freeze a winner into a forkable template).

**The principle behind all of them, straight from how we work:** the repetitive, high-volume grunt — pulling captions, extracting patterns, generating title variants — runs on **local Ollama**, free, in the background. The skill builds the prompt, launches it, hands you the result. *You* spend your judgment on the call, not the grind.

## PART 4 — THE LIVE PIPELINE BOARD (GitHub-fed, real)

There's a live kanban board showing exactly what the factory is building right now. It's not a screenshot or a status doc someone updates by hand — it's **fed directly from GitHub.**

How it works: a small backend (`factory_server.py`) calls the `gh` CLI against the real repos — issues from `rt-yt-substrate` and pull requests from both `rt-yt-substrate` and `rt-yt-knowledge`. It normalizes every issue into a kanban column by reading its labels:
- `status:in-progress` → **In Progress**
- `status:in-review` → **In Review**
- closed or `status:done` → **Done**
- everything else → **Todo**

It also pulls each PR's CI check status (passing / pending / failing) and links PRs back to the issues they close. The frontend (`factory-pipeline-live.html`) polls every ~4 seconds and animates the diffs, so the board *moves on its own* as the team opens issues, pushes PRs, and merges work. The goal banner up top reads **`$83K/mo run-rate · June 2027`** — the board never lets you forget the number.

**What this means for you:** the board is the single honest view of what's real vs. what's spec'd. When you wonder "is the upload automation built yet?" — you check the board, not a person. Run it locally with `python3 factory_server.py` (defaults to `http://127.0.0.1:8773`). Because it's GitHub-fed, **the way you move work on the board is by working in GitHub** — open an issue, label it, push a PR. The board reflects reality; it doesn't replace it.

## PART 5 — THE WINNER-LOG LOOP (how the portfolio gets smarter)

This is the most important habit in the whole system, so it gets its own section.

When a video over-indexes, the Chess Board doesn't just turn a cell red. It assembles a **Double-Down Card** that hands you the diagnosis and the next moves:

```
┌─ 🔴 DOUBLE DOWN NOW ──────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Channel: [aviation] "Cold War Interceptors"  (age: 31d)   │
│ Video:   "The MiG That Outran a Missile"                  │
│ Signal:  VI 6.2× baseline @ 72h · CTR 13.1% (+4.0pp)      │
│          APV 41% (+11pp) · 11,400 views in 72h            │
│ Quadrant:🟢 WINNING — replicate the cluster               │
│                                                           │
│ ▶ MAKE 3 MORE IN THIS CLUSTER (auto-generated):           │
│   1. "The Bomber That Outran Every Fighter Sent For It"   │
│   2. "The Spy Plane They Couldn't Shoot Down"             │
│   3. "The Dogfight That Broke the Speed Record"           │
│                                                           │
│ [ Log to winner-log ]  [ Assign + ship-by date ]         │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The card gives you the exact signal (velocity, CTR/APV lift, absolute views, channel age), the grid diagnosis in plain English, and **three auto-generated follow-up titles** in the winning vein. You hit **Log to winner-log** and it writes a pre-filled entry into `rt-yt-knowledge/winner-log/` — frontmatter, the signal, the diagnosis, and the three queued titles all filled in. You add the one thing only a human can: *why* it worked, and who ships the clones by when.

**Why this loop is sacred:** without it, every operator privately relearns the same lesson and the portfolio never compounds. With it, the moment *your* aviation video cracks a title formula, that lesson is in the shared corpus — and Sam's history channel, John's ai-dev channel, and every future channel can fork it. One operator's win becomes every operator's edge. That's the difference between 18 channels that each start from zero and 18 channels that share one rapidly-improving brain.

**The non-negotiable rule:** every over-indexer gets a winner-log entry. No exceptions. This is the mechanism that fills the brain. An empty winner-log is a portfolio that forgets.

## PART 6 — HOW YOU PLUG IN, DAY TO DAY

Concrete. Here's your actual rhythm.

### Every morning (5 minutes)
Open the **Chess Board dashboard**. In 30 seconds you should know: (a) is anything popping on my channels right now, (b) what's the diagnosis, (c) what 3 things do I make this week, (d) where am I against my YPP progress / run-rate / pay. Your **open Double-Down Cards are your to-do list.** If a red fired overnight, you'll also have been pinged in Slack — that's the interrupt; the dashboard is the durable view.

### When a card fires (the highest-value moment of your week)
Drop what you're doing. Read the quadrant. If it's 🟢 WINNING: hit **Log to winner-log**, take the three auto-generated titles (or write better ones), and queue 2–3 clones **this week**. Following the scent fast is the entire job. If it's a different quadrant, do the prescribed fix — re-package, fix the hook, or kill it. Don't improvise; the grid already told you the move.

### Making a video (your 1–4 hours of judgment, not labor)
1. Open your **topic queue** — approve a topic. (Don't research from scratch; the Swarm queued it.)
2. **Skim-edit the auto-drafted script** against your Bible. (Don't write from zero.)
3. Click **VO**. Open the **90%-built timeline**. Trim. (Don't build from an empty timeline.)
4. **Pick 1 of 3 thumbnails, 1 of 5 titles.** *Spend your real attention here* — this is the craft-of-intrigue gate and it controls your CTR.
5. **Publish.** (Manual today via Stage 09 — auto soon.)

Target operator touch time: **~45–90 min of judgment** on archival lanes, more on premium. If you can't get from approved-topic to a 90%-built timeline in under ~2 hours *without writing anything from scratch*, that's a **factory problem to flag, not an operator problem to grind through.** Tell Eric the manifest/auto-assembly needs fixing.

### When you research a competitor
Run **`/teardown`** against their channel. Review the auto-generated doc. Fill the steal list. The corpus just got smarter, and so did you.

### Your cadence and your cut
**2 videos/week/channel** is the rhythm — that's what banks watch-hours toward the YPP gate (1,000 subs + 4,000 hours) and gives the algorithm enough shots to find your winner. You own **~20% of your channels' upside.** Pre-YPP, the dashboard shows "$0 — banking watch hours" next to your YPP progress bar, so you can see the upside coming before the money lands. The speed you launch and the sharpness of your double-downs *are* your pay.

## THE WHOLE THING, IN ONE BREATH

Take a lot of cheap shots. Read the board like a chess game. The instant a video out-runs its own channel, log it and make three more — that week. The factory makes the shots cheap and the clones cheaper; the corpus makes every win permanent and shared; the dashboard makes the pop impossible to miss. Your judgment — which shot is hot, and what to make next — is the only scarce resource in the system. Everything else is plumbing built to free you up to play that one move well.

You're not making videos. You're manufacturing attention, reading where it's flowing, and pouring concrete on the channels the algorithm already wants to reward. Go find the pop.
